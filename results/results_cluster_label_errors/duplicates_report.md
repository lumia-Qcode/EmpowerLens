**Label conflicts (2).** 265 / 307 (similarity 1.0000): No Distortion vs Overgeneralization; 1496 / 2430 (similarity 0.8639): No Distortion vs Overgeneralization. Rows 265 and 307 are the same text, so at least one of the two labels is wrong. That is direct evidence of label noise in the source data. The 1496 / 2430 pair is the same post with a different stated country and age ("From the U.S.: I am 37" vs "From England: I am 16") and a few changed phrases (83% word overlap). Both pairs sit inside train.

**Cross-split pairs (2).** test 304 / train 336 (similarity 0.9905, both Fortune-telling); test 137 / train 139 (similarity 0.9749, both No Distortion). The original dataset contains each of these posts twice, with 98% of words in common. 304 / 336 differ only in "self harm" vs "self-harm" and two apostrophes. 137 / 139 differ only in "I'd" vs "idk" and a "(From India)" tag. So during training the model has effectively seen these two test rows, with their test labels. This breaks the Chapter 5 leakage rule (test case C-06 is recorded as a Fail). The frozen splits are not changed. Instead, the size of the effect is measured below.

## Headline test numbers with and without the two leaked rows

**Why these are bounds.** Per-row test predictions were not saved for the D13 or D09 runs, and the checkpoints were deleted after evaluation. So the 251-row numbers cannot be computed exactly. They are computed from the saved confusion counts instead. Row 137 (No Distortion) was either a true negative or a false positive, and row 304 (Fortune-telling) was either a true positive or a false negative. Every case the counts allow is tried. *Worst* and *best* are the extreme cases for each seed and each metric. For the multilabel model, each of the two rows may have fired any set of up to 2 types. That matches the `--max-labels 2` cap `src/evaluate.py` applied when D09 was evaluated. Row 304's true types: fortune_telling. The true 251-row value lies inside the range. Thresholds are the D13 binary thresholds (val FPR ≤ 0.15) and the D09 per-type thresholds. All of them were chosen on val, and nothing here was chosen on test.

Mean ± std over 3 seeds (population std, as in `docs/thresholds.md`). Test has 92 non-distorted and 161 distorted rows; 251 rows leaves 91 and 160.

| Test rows | Specificity | FPR | Sensitivity | Binary macro-F1 | Multilabel macro-F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All 253 | 0.768 ± 0.020 | 0.232 ± 0.020 | 0.667 ± 0.046 | 0.697 ± 0.021 | 0.247 ± 0.016 |
| 251, worst case | 0.766 ± 0.021 | 0.234 ± 0.021 | 0.665 ± 0.046 | 0.694 ± 0.021 | 0.243 ± 0.016 |
| 251, best case | 0.777 ± 0.021 | 0.223 ± 0.021 | 0.671 ± 0.046 | 0.703 ± 0.021 | 0.252 ± 0.016 |

Without the 2-type cap (if each row could have fired all 10 types), the multilabel 251-row range widens to 0.243 ± 0.016 – 0.258 ± 0.017.

Largest possible change in the 3-seed mean: Specificity 0.84 pt, FPR 0.84 pt, Sensitivity 0.42 pt, Binary macro-F1 0.56 pt, Multilabel macro-F1 0.46 pt.

**Size of the difference:** removing the two leaked test rows can move any headline mean by at most 0.84 percentage points (Specificity and FPR), which is smaller than the seed-to-seed std, so the leak does not change any conclusion. It is still reported in Chapter 9 as a limitation.
