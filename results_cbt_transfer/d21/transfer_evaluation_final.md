Model: **MentalRoBERTa** (`mental/mental-roberta-base`, D12 winner, `docs/model_selection.md`), multilabel, D12 recipe (lr 3e-5, 5 epochs, weight decay 0.1, max length 512, batch 16, `data/splits`), per-type thresholds fitted on val during training (`docs/thresholds.md`). No fine-tuning on CBT-Bench.

Data: `Psychotherapy-LLM/CBT-Bench`, config `distortions_test` (CBT-CD), 146 items, 0 dropped; labels mapped with the 4 Oct notebook's `CBT_LABEL_CANON`, copied verbatim (`results_cbt_transfer/kaggle-runner-transfer-eval.ipynb`, cell 4). 369 gold labels (1–3 per item).

Generated by `venv\Scripts\python.exe -m src.cbt_transfer_report` from `results_cbt_transfer/d21/cbt_per_class_<seed>.csv` (written by `notebooks/kaggle_runner_d21_cbt.ipynb`).

Seeds found: **42, 1337, 2024** (expected 42, 1337, 2024). Std is the sample std (ddof=1) over seeds.

## Read this first

- **Format difference:** CBT-CD gives each case as a *situation* plus an automatic *thought* (plus the original post, `ori_text`), not one free-written reflection like our training data. The headline table scores `ori_text`, as the 4 Oct run did; `situation + thoughts` is reported below as a second input.
- **Overlap with our data:** CBT-CD's 146 `ori_text` posts come from the same source as `Annotated_data.csv` (same Id_Numbers and text). Checked 20 Nov against the frozen splits: 110 items are in our **train** split, 18 in val, 18 in test. Only the *labels* are independent (CBT-Bench's own annotators and rules). So the 146-item score measures agreement with a different labelling of mostly-seen texts, not transfer to unseen text; the `not_in_train` subset (36 items) is the cleaner, much noisier check. The same overlap applied to the 4 Oct run.
- **Ties:** with about 15–65 items per class (21–65 here), a difference smaller than the seed std counts as a tie, not a win or loss. The 4 Oct column is a single seed (42) with a fixed 0.5 threshold and default training settings, so it has no std and is not the same recipe.
- Expect a big drop from our own test scores; that is normal across datasets (Chapter 3 cites 74.5% → 23.7% F1).
